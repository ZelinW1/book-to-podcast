# Audio workflow

## Ingest and plan

`python scripts/ingest_book.py BOOK --output PROJECT/source` extracts EPUB in `spine` order and reads its navigation table when present. PDF uses the document table of contents and page locations; OCR only pages with no useful text, and report pages whose layout or OCR is uncertain. DOCX uses heading styles. Other text formats use headings where possible. Do not treat page breaks or EPUB file boundaries as definitive episode boundaries.

Read `source/index.md` first. It lists order, titles, sizes, and chunk IDs without including the full book. Inspect only selected `source/chunks/*.txt` files. For long works, produce a compact chapter summary and a series outline; retain only those and relevant chunks while writing an episode. Cache completed sections and source hashes so reruns do not repeat extraction or synthesis.

Agree on adaptation density before writing: overview (major thesis or plot), chapter guide (every chapter's main steps), close reading (most substantive claims, examples, objections, and turns). A user may specify another density. Build `series-plan.md` as a coverage table mapping every substantive chapter or section to an episode, its role in the work, planned treatment, any proposed omissions, and estimated spoken minutes. Do not infer episode count from page count alone or collapse a whole book into one generic summary. Use a representative voice sample to estimate words or characters per minute and state the approximate total runtime. Agree on a target and any hard maximum for this work. Treat duration as a listening constraint: split or reorganize a dense episode without dropping an important step, and never increase speech speed merely to fit the limit. A short front matter section may be omitted from speech with a note.

For each episode, outline the source's progression before drafting. In nonfiction, follow the author's question, definitions, distinctions, examples or evidence, objections, replies, and conclusion in order; preserve qualifications and distinguish the author's claims from the host's explanation. In fiction, preserve the narrative order and avoid later spoilers. Add a transition only where it helps listening, and label a newly invented illustration as the narrator's own. Check that every item promised in the coverage table has a corresponding script passage or a documented omission.

## Script and source checks

Use `references/script-format.md` exactly for machine-readable speech turns. Make each `##` turn a coherent spoken unit, usually one or two paragraphs; tune synthesis chunks separately to the chosen model's reliable length. Keep source IDs in HTML comments, which are not spoken. The Markdown itself is the editable source of truth.

Write `adaptation-notes.md` for each episode: covered chunk IDs, concepts retained, compression and omissions, uncertain passages, pronunciation choices, and any original examples added for explanation. Check names, dates, quotations, distinctions, and conclusions against the cited chunks. An example invented for clarity must be introduced as an example, not attributed to the book.

Build a script pilot with an opening and a difficult representative passage, both with cited source notes. Review it with the user before drafting the full series. Compare its order and detail to the cited source and the chosen density; correct the series outline if it would make later episodes too generic.

After every coherent section, read it aloud or listen to a rough take and ask: Does it sound like a person explaining an idea rather than reading a paper? Can a new listener identify the current question, the terms in play, and why the next move follows? Is there room to absorb a hard distinction before another arrives? Are narrator-made analogies marked as such, and do they preserve the author's qualifications? If any answer fails, reorganize and rewrite, then verify the revised claim against its source. A useful pattern for a difficult move is question, plain-language foothold, concrete illustration, author's move, and the question it leaves open; vary it to fit the text. Keep useful complexity, but remove needless jargon and sentence-by-sentence retelling. Avoid filler added only to reach a runtime.

Before synthesis, audit all scripts against the coverage map and source notes. Check conceptual transitions, pronounceability, sentence length, and estimated duration at the auditioned pace. The user should review the complete scripts and notes before full-series synthesis unless one-pass production was explicitly requested.

If the request language and detected book language differ, ask before translating. If the source language is uncertain, inspect representative chunks before deciding.

## Synthesis

Use the TTS engine selected by the user. When unspecified and no local model is configured, present a short choice: an available local model, a lightweight local model such as Piper where the language has a suitable voice, or `edge-tts` as an online alternative. Never represent `edge-tts` as offline. Confirm engine, voice, and delivery style once, then reuse them across an episode or series unless the script intentionally changes speaker or style. The entrypoint links optional engine-specific instructions.

Audition the chosen narrator on actual script text before a costly run. Include a calm introduction, a longer dense passage, and at least two joins. Ask whether a listener new to the topic can follow the difficult passage at first hearing; voice similarity alone is insufficient. Settle the voice or reference, pronunciation, pace, phrase breaks, pauses, and delivery from that sample, and obtain the user's approval before full synthesis. If it feels rushed, first revise dense sentences and punctuation, then adjust model controls, voice reference, or chunking and meaningful pauses. Avoid aggressive time stretching, blanket slowdowns that sound unnatural, or speeding up to meet a duration target. Avoid an exaggerated promotional tone for a long listening session unless requested.

For speech assembled from segments, keep the same role-to-voice mapping, reference audio or prompt when applicable, decoding settings, pace, and style guidance at every boundary. Choose chunks long enough for natural prosody but within the model's reliable length; test a longer chunk if short chunks sound disjointed, while checking for drift, omissions, or repetitions. Sentence and paragraph boundaries are preferable to arbitrary character cuts. Listen across joins in the assembled audio: an unintended change in timbre, accent, energy, or pacing is a defect even when each segment sounds acceptable alone. Re-synthesize mismatched segments or revise chunking before delivery; loudness matching and crossfades cannot repair a different performance. If a different engine is needed, compare it on the same passage and joins before switching the series.

Check actual episode duration against the agreed target and hard maximum, along with intelligibility, missing or repeated speech, and long silences. Listen for sustained rushed delivery and insufficient thinking space, especially in dense passages; repair the script or synthesis before delivery. Use ASR or another transcript comparison for coverage when available, then listen to flagged spans and representative joins. Preserve segment audio and timing so corrections do not require rebuilding an entire episode. `timing.json` from synthesis records segment boundaries; it does not establish accurate word or subtitle cue times. If subtitles are requested, align the final mixed narration to the script or corrected transcript with a forced aligner, verify cue order and coverage, and inspect the opening, middle of long chunks, joins, and ending by listening with subtitles.

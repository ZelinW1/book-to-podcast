# Video workflow

Use the final episode audio and verified subtitle cues to build a video with code-drawn frames and a reproducible render command. Use `timing.json` for synthesis segment boundaries or speaker metadata only. Choose the visual concept from the audience, subject, platform, user preferences, and available assets. Set the aspect ratio, book and episode title layout, author/translator credits, episode numbering, character treatment, subtitle style, background art, music, opening voice buffer, and a standalone 4:3 series cover in the approved plan. Credit wording and episode labels are editorial choices, not fixed defaults. Use licensed or original visual and music assets and retain attribution where required. Possible elements include typography, chapter cards, diagrams, illustrations, source quotations, a waveform, or speaker indicators. Use only elements that clarify the episode; do not let the included sample renderer define the video's structure or look.

`scripts/render_video.py` is an optional minimal starter: it draws a title, progress, audio activity, and verified captions when `--subtitles` is supplied, using Pillow and FFmpeg. Pass `--font` with a font that covers the episode language. Adapt the starter or create a different renderer when the brief calls for another layout, aspect ratio, motion style, subtitle treatment, or visual material. Keep narration and visual timing aligned. Do not use a graphical editing application as the implementation path.

Create editable SRT or equivalent cues by aligning the final narration to its transcript. Do not split a long synthesis segment by character count or uniformly distribute its time: speaking pace varies within a segment and can make middle captions lead or lag badly. Reject truncated or unreadably long captions. Spot-check cue changes against speech at the opening, a dense middle passage, at least one segment join, and the ending. Apply any opening voice buffer to the narration, subtitles, and duration together. For video playback, a short buffer of roughly 1–2 seconds is a useful starting choice when the user has not specified one; keep the first spoken syllable clear after decoding. By default, add gentle, natural background music unless the user opts out. Use original, generated, or appropriately licensed material. It should be perceptible but clearly below the narration, with smooth entry, looping or transitions, and exit. Listen on ordinary speakers to the opening, normal and quiet speech, joins, and ending; numerical loudness alone does not establish a good balance. Check the complete rendered duration so a short preview loop or temporary track does not leave the later video silent. Music must not mask consonants or make a dense passage harder to follow.

After the videos, render one separate 4:3 series cover by default, unless the user requests another format or no cover. Carry over the final visual language and include the book title, author, and main illustration or portrait when used in the videos. Verify the exact aspect ratio, spelling, image quality, and readability at thumbnail size. Keep the cover source and assets with the project.

Preserve the rendering code, settings, source script, audio and timing references, subtitles, fonts and other assets needed for reproduction, and final video. Store assets with the project or document stable paths; the starter's `video-project.json` records absolute paths and does not package them automatically.

Before rendering the full series, review static previews for each episode's title and short encoded samples covering the opening, dense middle, join, and ending. Confirm title/credit spelling, subtitle legibility and synchronization, character placement, music level, and the first spoken syllable. Ask the user to review the encoded pilot before batch rendering unless they explicitly requested one-pass production or already approved the same scope. After rendering, verify the requested file count, each video's full decode, audio and video durations, cue coverage, opening buffer, full-duration music, and representative frames. Check that every final video covers its complete episode unless the user requested an excerpt; retain the editable cues and render settings. Verify and deliver the standalone cover as its own file.
